Multimodal Fiction Research

Image as Narrative Influence

Research notes on using visual information not merely to describe an image, but to influence tone, behavior, pacing, scene logic, and generated prose.

1. The Core Idea

Conventional image-to-text systems primarily answer: “What is in this image?” The proposed experiment asks a different question: “What should this image make the writing do?”

The image is treated as an influence source, not simply a caption source. Visual information can be converted into structured signals that participate in a fiction, character, or behavior engine before text is generated.

2. Existing Research Landscape

Image Captioning Converts visual content into literal language descriptions. Useful as a semantic layer, but does not by itself provide deep narrative steering.
Grounded Story Generation Uses images plus prompts to generate text consistent with visible characters, settings, objects, and events.
Visual Storytelling Uses image sequences as narrative prompts and attempts to maintain event and character continuity across the resulting story.
Visual Influence The experimental direction here: extract semantic and aesthetic properties and use them to alter generation behavior, tone, pacing, and character reactions.

Related research includes visually grounded story generation, visual writing prompts, multimodal long-story generation, and multimodal visual storytelling systems.

8. Image Does Not Override Character Data

The image should function as evidence or influence, while the character model remains authoritative for identity and established behavior. This is especially important for a fictional behavior engine.

Image evidenceProvides current visual information and influence.

12. Research Questions

RQ1Can visual embeddings reliably influence prose without merely causing image description?
RQ2Can aesthetic properties be separated from semantic content?
RQ3Can visual influence modify behavior while preserving character identity?
RQ4Can multiple images create measurable narrative transitions?
RQ5Can image and audio influences be combined without one modality overwhelming the other?
RQ6Can influence weights be exposed and manually adjusted by a writer?

13. Practical Prototype

A first prototype does not require training a new multimodal model.

  1. Load an image.
  2. Generate a vision embedding and/or structured visual analysis.
  3. Compare the embedding against a library of textual influence concepts.
  4. Build a weighted visual influence profile.
  5. Combine that profile with character, relationship, transformation, and scene data.
  6. Give the resulting state to an LLM as a structured control layer.
  7. Generate the scene.
  8. Optionally compare the generated result against the original influence profile.

14. Experimental Control

A useful test is to hold the story seed and character data constant while changing only the image. If the resulting prose changes in predictable ways, the experiment has measurable evidence that the visual input is functioning as an influence rather than merely being quoted or described. 

CONSTANT
  Character = Rainee
  Scene seed = "Rainee encounters Noir in Jade City."
  Personality = fixed
  Rules = fixed

VARIABLE
  Image A → comedic / energetic
  Image B → ominous / dark
  Image C → elegant / quiet
  Image D → surreal / chaotic

COMPARE
  tone
  pacing
  dialogue density

  scene escalation

15. Research Direction

The strongest conceptual direction is to treat images as one modality inside a broader multimodal evidence and influence system. The goal is not to replace the fiction engine with a vision model. The goal is to give the fiction engine another source of structured evidence that can affect its decisions.

Working concept:
Image → representation → influence profile → scene/behavior computation → language.

This is the visual counterpart to the audio experiment: sound should influence what the story feels like; images should influence what the story sees, feels like, and does.

16. Related Research Areas

Areas to investigate further include CLIP and vision-language embeddings, image captioning, visually grounded story generation, visual writing prompts, multimodal long-story generation, visual storytelling, multimodal LLMs, representation steering, activation steering, and cross-modal alignment.
